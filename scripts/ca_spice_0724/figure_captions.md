**Figure 1. CA-SPICE pipeline for dynamic ³¹P MRSI of the calf at 3T.**
(a) Acquisition: 2D concentric-ring (CRT) k-space trajectory, repeated throughout a rest–exercise–recovery protocol (≈1 min rest, 5 min exercise, ≈6 min recovery), giving 10–14 s per spectroscopic image at 20–30 mm in-plane voxel size.
(b) Prior information. Spatial: gastrocnemius (GM) and soleus masks drawn on the localizer define a compartment label for every CSI voxel (GM, soleus, other muscle); the compartment-aware smoothing graph connects neighbouring voxels within the same compartment, weighted by an 80-ring PCr reference image, and cuts all edges across compartment boundaries (red dotted). Spectral: simulated ³¹P muscle spectra are compressed by SVD into a low-rank spectral basis.
(c) CA-SPICE reconstructs all voxels jointly from the k-space data under both constraints.
(d) Output: resting PCr map, rest vs end-of-exercise spectrum and the full dynamic series (one spectrum per repetition) of the marked GM voxel.

**Figure 2. NUFFT vs CA-SPICE spectra at the finest (MID00283, 20 mm, 14 s/rep) and coarsest (MID00279, 30 mm, 10 s/rep) resolution.**
Middle: spectra of all GM (green outline) and soleus (blue outline) voxels on the localizer with the CSI grid (cyan), for NUFFT and CA-SPICE; rest (black, mean of the rest repetitions) and end of exercise (red, mean of the last 4 repetitions inside exercise), one y-scale per map. Left / right: enlarged spectra of one GM voxel and of the soleus voxel directly above it (dashed outlines, arrows). Numbers give the end-of-exercise minus rest change of the PCr and Pi peak heights, as % of the resting PCr peak. Magnitude spectra, 8 Hz line broadening, PCr = 0 ppm. Both methods agree in GM; in soleus, CA-SPICE shows a smaller PCr depletion and Pi increase than NUFFT, most clearly at 20 mm, consistent with less gastrocnemius signal blurred into the soleus voxels.

**Figure 3. PCr depletion and Pi increase maps, MID00283 (20 mm voxels, 14 s/rep).**
End of exercise (mean of the last 4 repetitions inside exercise) vs rest (mean of the rest repetitions), from voxel peak heights: PCr depletion = 100·(1 − PCr_ex/PCr_rest); Pi increase = 100·(Pi_ex − Pi_rest)/PCr_rest. Green / blue outlines: GM / soleus. Voxels with high repetition-to-repetition PCr noise, < 50 % muscle content, or adjacent to the reference phantom are not shown (same voxels for both methods and in Fig. 4). Dashed: the voxels shown in Fig. 2. NUFFT gives similar values in GM and soleus; CA-SPICE separates a strongly responding gastrocnemius (PCr −49 %, Pi +29, medians) from a weakly responding soleus (PCr −21 %, Pi +6).

**Figure 4. Repeatability across four exercise bouts with different protocols (20–30 mm voxels, 10–14 s/rep).**
Left: GM and soleus ROI time courses of all four bouts, aligned at exercise onset (grey: exercise), for NUFFT and CA-SPICE. Solid: PCr peak height relative to rest; dashed: change of the Pi peak height from rest, in units of resting PCr. ROI spectrum = phase-aligned mean of the included voxels (exclusion as in Fig. 3), noise-power-corrected magnitude, 5-repetition moving mean within each phase. Right: end-of-exercise PCr depletion and Pi increase of each bout vs voxel size (from the averaged ROI spectra, as in Figs. 2–3); filled / solid: GM, open / dashed: soleus; values are mean ± SD over the four bouts. GM responses are reproducible and identical with both methods (PCr −44 ± 4 % NUFFT, −49 ± 4 % CA-SPICE; Pi +29 ± 7, +28 ± 6). CA-SPICE separates soleus from GM consistently in every bout (soleus PCr −26 ± 6 %, Pi +9 ± 7), whereas NUFFT soleus values follow GM (PCr −37 ± 5 %, Pi +22 ± 6), approaching GM at 30 mm.
