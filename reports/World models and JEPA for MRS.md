# Latent prediction can guide SPICE, not replace it

World models and JEPA are two answers to one question: what should a learned model of a system be required to predict? A world model is a state estimator plus a transition model (optionally with an observation decoder), which is the structure of a Kalman filter with the matrices learned; JEPA is the variant that deletes the decoder and predicts only the *embedding* of the missing or future signal, so that the encoder is free to discard whatever is unpredictable. That design choice decides the answer for MRS/MRSI reconstruction. An inverse-problem prior must supply a decoder, a denoiser, a scalar energy, or a projector, and a plain JEPA supplies none of them, so **a JEPA cannot replace the subspace reconstruction in this repository**; the research notes found no published work applying JEPA or world models to MRS, MRSI, 31P or NMR, and no JEPA used as a reconstruction prior in any MR modality. First-principles analysis also shows that linear latent prediction on a noiseless FID reduces to the Prony/HSVD shift-invariance structure that MRS already exploits, and that under noise it keeps strong, narrow resonances first and weak or broad ones last, which is the wrong ordering for low-concentration metabolites and for the pH-dependent Pi peak. The ideas still earn a place in three roles that leave data consistency intact: a temporal state-space ("world") model for the dynamic 31P script, which currently reconstructs every repetition independently; a learned similarity or out-of-distribution signal that steers the existing linear solver without making it nonlinear; and a simulation-pretrained encoder given a decoder and used as a nonlinear manifold constraint beside the SVD basis. The first two preserve the exact Gaussian posterior on which the repository's uncertainty quantification depends, and that is the criterion that should rank the options. One caveat on evidence: the notes were compiled with web search only, so most sources were checked at abstract level, and several derivations below are inferences of the researchers and are labelled as such.

## A world model is a learned Kalman filter; the open question is what it must predict

### State, transition, observation: the same graph from 1960 to Dreamer

A world model is an internal model of an environment with three parts: an encoder that infers a compact state from the observation history, a transition model p(s_{t+1} | s_t, a_t) that advances the state under an action or control input, and optionally an observation model p(o_t | s_t) and a reward model. This is the partially observed Markov decision process of control theory, in which the agent maintains a belief state updated by a Bayes filter ([Kaelbling et al. 1998](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0004-3702(98)00023-X)). The Kalman filter is the exact solution when dynamics and measurement are linear and noise is Gaussian: x_{t+1} = A x_t + B u_t + w_t, y_t = C x_t + v_t, alternating a predict step and an update step ([Kalman 1960](https://doi.org/10.1115/1.3662552)). Deep state-space models keep that graph and replace A and C with neural networks and exact inference with amortised variational inference ([Krishnan et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.05121)). The canonical modern form, PlaNet's recurrent state-space model, has a posterior q(s_t | s_{t-1}, a_{t-1}, o_t), a prior p(s_t | s_{t-1}, a_{t-1}), an observation model and a reward model, trained on a variational bound that is a reconstruction term minus a KL term between posterior and prior ([Hafner et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.04551)). The free-energy principle from neuroscience states the same objective with the sign reversed ([Friston 2010](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn2787)).

"World model" therefore names a role, not an architecture. A Kalman filter with hand-specified matrices is a world model with a physics prior and no learned parameters; an RSSM is the same model with learned nonlinear maps. Ha and Schmidhuber's 2018 paper, which popularised the term, split the agent into a VAE that compresses each frame, a recurrent network that predicts the next latent, and a small controller that can be trained entirely inside the model's "dream" ([Ha & Schmidhuber 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.10122)). The Dreamer line scaled that recipe to a single configuration across more than 150 tasks ([DreamerV3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104)) and, in 2025, to an agent that obtains Minecraft diamonds purely from offline data ([Dreamer 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24527)). There is theoretical backing for why such models matter: any agent that generalises across a sufficiently broad set of multi-step goals must contain a predictive model of its environment that can be extracted from its policy ([Richens et al., ICML 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/richens25a.html)). Surveys through 2026 nevertheless agree that the term has no consensus definition, dividing roughly into "understanding" (representation) and "predicting" (simulation) readings ([Ding et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14499)).

### Three choices of prediction target

The decisive design axis is what the model is trained to predict. Generative world models predict observations: Dreamer grounds its latents by reconstruction, and interactive video models such as Genie 3 render navigable 720p worlds at 24 fps ([Google DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/genie-3-a-new-frontier-for-world-models/)). Value-equivalent models predict only task quantities: MuZero learns dynamics trained solely on reward, value and policy and never reconstructs an observation ([Schrittwieser et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08265)). JEPA models sit between them and predict a learned representation of the future or missing signal.

The argument for leaving observation space is that observations carry a great deal of information that is unpredictable or irrelevant, and a reconstruction loss forces capacity onto it. For natural images, the subspace that explains most pixel variance is nearly the least useful for classification ([Balestriero & LeCun 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11337)). The argument against is that reconstruction is a dense, collapse-free training signal, and it produces an inspectable output; the Dreamer family, which uses it, holds the broadest single-configuration results. Neither camp has won, and the cautionary literature applies to both: video diffusion models trained on a physics simulator generalise by mimicking the closest training case and fail out of distribution ([Kang et al., ICML 2025](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/poster/46015)), and a transformer that predicts orbits accurately does not recover Newtonian gravitation when probed ([Vafa et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06952)). For a scientific application this is the central warning. Accurate prediction inside the training regime does not certify that the latent state corresponds to the physics.

## JEPA selects features by predictability, and that cuts both ways

### The architecture is an energy with a trivial minimum

LeCun's 2022 position paper defines JEPA as an energy-based model. Two encoders produce s_x = Enc(x) and s_y = Enc(y); a predictor maps s_x, with optional conditioning or latent variable z, toward s_y; the energy is the distance D(s_y, Pred(s_x, z)). Low energy means y is a compatible completion or continuation of x. The model is explicitly not generative, since nothing maps back to y ([LeCun 2022](https://openreview.net/forum?id=BZ5a1r-kVsf)). I-JEPA is the canonical instance: a context encoder sees visible image patches, a target encoder (an exponential moving average of the context encoder) sees the full image, and a narrow predictor regresses the target-patch representations with a squared-error loss, with no pixel decoder and no hand-crafted augmentations ([Assran et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08243)). JEPA differs from masked autoencoders only in where the loss lives, and from invariance-based methods such as BYOL or VICReg in that the predictor is conditioned on what differs between the views (mask position, time offset, action), so the embedding need not be invariant.

Because both ends of the regression are learned, mapping every input to a constant gives zero loss. Preventing this collapse is JEPA's central engineering problem, and the fixes form a hierarchy of rigour. A stop-gradient with an EMA teacher works empirically and has theory only for linear networks ([Tian et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.06810)). VICReg adds explicit variance and covariance penalties, which constrain second moments only ([Bardes et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.04906)). LeJEPA argues that an isotropic Gaussian is the embedding distribution that minimises downstream prediction risk and enforces it with a single regulariser, SIGReg, removing the teacher-student heuristics ([Balestriero & LeCun 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08544)). LeWorldModel carries that regulariser to an action-conditioned world model with two loss terms and about 15M parameters, trainable on one GPU in a few hours ([Maes et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19312v1)). That scale matters here, because it puts a principled JEPA within reach of a small lab working from simulated data. A fourth option sidesteps the problem by freezing a pretrained encoder, as DINO-WM does ([Zhou et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04983)).

### One theorem: JEPA orders features by regression coefficient, MAE by variance

The claim that JEPA "ignores noise" has exactly one rigorous backing. In deep linear self-distillation networks, Littwin and colleagues show that both objectives learn features greedily, but **the order is set by the regression coefficient ρ for JEPA and by the covariance λ for a masked autoencoder**; JEPA favours "influential" features, MAE favours high-variance features even when they are noisy, and the JEPA bias appears only when the encoder is deep ([Littwin et al., NeurIPS 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.03475)). The empirical counterpart is I-JEPA's own ablation, in which moving the loss to pixel space degraded linear-probe accuracy.

The consequence, an inference in the notes, is that JEPA selects by predictability and is indifferent to amplitude. A small-amplitude feature that is deterministically related between context and target is favoured relative to MAE, and anti-collapse whitening lets it occupy a full embedding dimension. A feature that is important but weakly predictable from the context is learned last or not at all.

The same bias has a documented failure mode. In a moving-dot testbed, JEPA matches or beats reconstruction when distractor noise changes every step and **fails when the distractor is fixed in time**, because a static nuisance is perfectly predictable and therefore the easiest thing to represent ([Sobal et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.10831)). Several 2026 preprints describe the same "informational collapse", in which variance and rank statistics look healthy while fast or causal factors are dropped ([MotionJEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23881)). Three further limits bear on quantitative use. JEPA has no native path to signal space, and every published visualisation trains a decoder afterwards on frozen features. Mainstream JEPA predictors are deterministic and carry no uncertainty ([arXiv:2611.09302](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.09302)). And dense, fine-detail features do not emerge reliably from mask-only latent prediction, which is why V-JEPA 2.1 had to add a loss on visible tokens ([Mur-Labadia et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14482)). On 3D brain MRI, the one head-to-head found, MAE-based pretraining generally outperformed baseline JEPA for disease detection ([arXiv:2606.13315](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13315)).

## On an FID, linear latent prediction rediscovers Prony and inherits its low-SNR bias

### What a reconstruction prior must provide, and what JEPA provides instead

MRSI measures the spatial Fourier transform of a spatiospectral function, modulated by B0-induced phase and buried in noise; the target is high-dimensional and the data are few and noisy ([Lam, Peng & Liang 2023](https://unpaywall.org/10.1109%2FMSP.2022.3215288)). Every mainstream way of injecting a learned prior into such a problem requires one of four objects, as the table shows.

| Route | Object the prior must supply | Does a plain JEPA supply it? |
|---|---|---|
| Plug-and-play, RED, diffusion posterior sampling | Denoiser or score | No |
| Generative-model compressed sensing | Decoder G(z) with low-dimensional latent | No |
| Learned regulariser | Scalar energy R(x) with a usable gradient | Only by construction (prediction error, or a density estimate) |
| Subspace or manifold constraint | Basis, autoencoder, or projector | No (encoder only) |

A JEPA provides an encoder f and a latent predictor g, so one of the four objects has to be built from them. The notes identify three constructions. The first is a prediction-error energy, E(x) = Σ ‖g(f(x_context)) − f(x_target)‖² over maskings, which is low when parts of the signal are mutually predictable. The second is a density estimate: the anti-collapse term of a trained JEPA is claimed to estimate the data density, recoverable from the encoder Jacobian ([Balestriero et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05949)). The third is a bolted-on decoder, as in Representation Autoencoders, which pair a frozen self-supervised encoder with a trained decoder ([Zheng et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11690)). Only the third has empirical precedent, and it carries a warning: frozen semantic encoders reconstruct natural images at roughly 19 to 23 dB PSNR ([DecQ](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22777)), so fine detail is lost. The nearest published bridge to inverse problems uses self-supervised features as an auxiliary alignment term on top of a diffusion prior, not as the prior itself ([Align & Invert](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16870)).

A feature-space penalty also fails as a complete prior for a structural reason. A term λ‖f(x) − t‖² constrains only the directions the encoder is sensitive to, and a JEPA encoder is trained to be insensitive to unpredictable content. The reconstruction is determined only where the null space of the acquisition operator and the null space of the encoder Jacobian do not overlap. Noise-like and weak-peak directions are the ones the encoder ignores, so the penalty leaves them to data consistency alone. That avoids hallucination in those directions and equally provides no denoising of them.

### The derivation: predictor eigenvalues are the Prony poles

The notes derive a direct link between linear JEPA and classical MRS signal processing. No published version of it was found, so it is an inference, though it rests on elementary linear algebra. A noiseless FID is a sum of K damped complex exponentials, x[n] = Σ a_k z_k^n; it obeys an order-K linear recurrence and its Hankel matrix has rank K, which is the basis of HSVD quantification ([Barkhuijsen et al. 1987](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-2364(87)90023-0)). Delay-embed the FID into vectors h_n of length L. Every h_n lies in the K-dimensional range of a Vandermonde matrix V, and h_{n+1} = (V Z V⁺) h_n with Z = diag(z_k). A linear JEPA minimises Σ ‖P E h_n − E h_{n+1}‖² over encoder E and predictor P. If E is injective on the signal subspace, P = (EV) Z (EV)⁻¹ achieves zero loss, and **the eigenvalues of the predictor are exactly the poles z_k**, that is, the frequencies and dampings. This is the shift-invariance equation of HSVD and the operator of Hankel dynamic mode decomposition, which converges to Koopman eigenvalues ([Arbabi & Mezić 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.06664)). An FID is, in this language, a finite-dimensional Koopman-invariant signal, and a JEPA with a linear predictor is a Koopman autoencoder without its decoder. The result is consistent with published claims that idealised JEPA losses are minimised by Koopman-invariant subspaces ([arXiv:2511.09783](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.09783)), although that paper treats regime indicators with eigenvalue one and advocates a near-identity predictor, which would be the wrong inductive bias for oscillating, decaying modes.

Two caveats follow from the same algebra. First, the solution is not unique: any encoder that keeps only a subset of the modes also achieves zero loss, so the prediction loss never forces all K resonances to survive. Which ones do is decided by the anti-collapse term and the learning dynamics. Second, amplitudes are not identifiable. The poles are invariant to reparametrising the latent, but the a_k, and hence concentrations, cannot be read from (E, P) without a decoder or a probe.

Noise then determines which modes survive. With additive noise, regressing a mode's coordinate at n+1 on its coordinate at n gives a coefficient shrunk to about z_k · SNR_k / (1 + SNR_k), the errors-in-variables effect behind the known low-SNR bias of linear prediction. Combined with the Littwin ordering, modes are prioritised by |z_k| · SNR_k / (1 + SNR_k): **strong, slowly decaying resonances first; weak or broad resonances last or never**. White noise has zero regression coefficient and is rejected, which is what SVD truncation already does. Highly predictable nuisances (baseline, residual water or lipid in 1H, fixed spurious signals, coil-specific structure) are the static distractor of Sobal et al. and are favoured over low-concentration metabolites. This ordering was derived from linear theory and from natural-image evidence. No study has measured JEPA retention of low-amplitude components on any spectroscopic signal.

## Where the ideas help this 31P SPICE pipeline, and where they break it

### The baseline is linear, simulated and exactly quantified

The repository is a classical physics-based pipeline with no neural component. The spectral basis V is the leading right singular vectors of synthetic training FIDs (5000 by default, rank 6 in the documented example), the forward model is a NUFFT with a per-voxel B0 phase matrix, spatial regularisation is a weighted graph Laplacian, and the normal equations are solved by conjugate gradients (`/home/user/SPICE_Margarita_31P/scripts/05e_spice_dynamic_31p.py`, `/home/user/SPICE_Margarita_31P/utils/recon.py`). A joint solver supports several rank blocks with separate regularisation, a union-of-subspaces formulation. Fitting uses FSL-MRS with a basis of PCr, Pi, PDE and J-coupled ATP multiplets (`/home/user/SPICE_Margarita_31P/scripts/specfitting/make_31p_basis.py`). Because the reconstruction is linear in the data once V is fixed, the posterior is Gaussian and the pipeline computes its covariance exactly and propagates it through fitting (`/home/user/SPICE_Margarita_31P/README.md`). This follows SPICE with learned subspaces, in which the basis comes from training data in place of subject-specific navigators ([Lam et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6824949)).

Two properties of that baseline frame everything else. Its bias is easy to characterise: it comes only from signal outside span(V) and from the spatial regulariser. And 31P is a sparse spectrum of roughly 7 to 12 resonances, so a low-rank linear model is already very effective. The dominant problem is SNR and resolution, with nominal 7T brain voxels still around 9.1 × 9.1 × 16 mm³ ([Wiesner et al., ISMRM 2021](https://archive.ismrm.org/2021/0235.html)). Reported gains from subspace methods in 31P are SNR gains, such as more than threefold noise reduction in NAD mapping ([ISMRM 2023 #3291](https://cds.ismrm.org/protected/23MProceedings/PDFfiles/3291_Zb73vCRBK.html)).

### Three places a learned predictive model plausibly helps

The strongest opening is the time axis of dynamic acquisitions. The dynamic script computes one shared subspace and then "solves the SPICE normal equation per rep independently", so no model links consecutive frames. A dynamic 31P experiment is the MRS setting that most resembles a world-model problem: a low-dimensional physiological state (PCr, Pi, pH) evolves under a known intervention (exercise, recovery, RF saturation), and each frame is a noisy partial observation of it. Existing work models this axis with low-rank tensors, a linear temporal subspace ([Ma et al. 2017](https://eld.bl.uk/catalog/100047913544_0x000001)), and the notes found no learned predictive dynamics model for PCr kinetics. The right tool is the decoder-bearing end of the world-model family. Quantified amplitudes are the output of interest, so a Kalman-type or latent-ODE model that maps state to signal fits better than a decoder-free JEPA. Learned Kalman gains with partially known dynamics already exist as a template ([KalmanNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.10043)).

The second opening is nonlinear spectral variation that a global linear basis represents inefficiently. Linewidth and frequency shift enter the signal nonlinearly, so a nonlinear model needs fewer dimensions than a linear subspace for the same accuracy; this is the argument behind autoencoder-constrained MRSI reconstruction ([Lam et al., IEEE TMI 2020](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Constrained-Magnetic-Resonance-Spectroscopic-Imaging-by/9984446409702771)) and RAIISE's learned projector ([Li et al. 2025](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/joint-learning-of-nonlinear-representation-and-projection-for-fas/)). Combining a linear subspace with a learned manifold projector "effectively regularizes the subspace fitting, especially at higher orders" ([ISMRM 2023 #0870](https://cds.ismrm.org/protected/23MProceedings/PDFfiles/0870_OaUvAfRLS.html)). In 31P the relevant nonlinearities are B0 shifts, the pH-dependent Pi shift and lineshape distortion. A JEPA-pretrained encoder could occupy the encoder slot of such a scheme. Once it is given a decoder, though, the method is an autoencoder-constrained reconstruction with a different pretraining objective, and no head-to-head comparison of the two pretraining objectives as manifold constraints exists.

The third opening is amortisation and pooling. Per FID, model-based fitting is already governed by Cramér-Rao bounds, and a learned model does not beat them. What it can add is a shared lifted space across voxels and subjects in which lineshape distortions become simple, or a learned similarity that decides which voxels should share information. Weak peaks become estimable only by pooling across voxels, so a better pooling rule is a real gain. The same embedding could support the density-based outlier detection proposed for JEPA, flagging voxels where the prior should be down-weighted. That use carries lower risk than using the model as the regulariser.

### Four ways it fails for quantitative 31P

The first failure is the selection bias derived above. The scientifically interesting 31P signals are small, broad or atypical: NAD, PME and PDE changes, a split Pi peak, the Pi chemical shift that encodes pH, PCr depletion during exercise. A predictability-ordered encoder ranks them last. The MRS literature has already documented the same pattern for supervised networks: deep-learning quantification appears more precise than model fitting at low SNR because uncertain cases are biased toward the training-set average ([Rizzo et al. 2023](https://boris.unibe.ch/176145)), and denoised spectra that look better "do not help quantitative evaluations" because noise removal is more efficient in signal-free regions ([Dziadosz et al. 2023](https://boris.unibe.ch/183536/1/Magnetic_Resonance_in_Med_-_2023_-_Dziadosz_-_Denoising_single_MR_spectra_by_deep_learning_Miracle_or_mirage.pdf)). At low SNR, the "unpredictable" content that JEPA discards includes the true deviation of a voxel from the population.

The second failure is hallucination through the decoder. Any post-hoc decoder on frozen JEPA features must invent whatever the embedding dropped; in I-JEPA's own visualisations, what varies between decoded samples is the information absent from the representation. Hallucination in learned reconstruction is tied to the kernel of the forward operator and is encouraged by standard training ([Gottschling et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.01258)). A prior that fails to represent weak peaks cannot pool them, and one that represents them too confidently inserts them.

The third failure is loss of the exact posterior. Any nonlinear prior inside the reconstruction breaks the linear-Gaussian structure that makes the repository's covariance exact, and data-driven estimators have no validated counterpart to Cramér-Rao bounds ([review of machine learning for 1H-MRS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.09621)). Deterministic JEPA predictors add no uncertainty of their own.

The fourth is data. MRSI deep learning already trains on tens of subjects, and one diffusion study built its own set of 25 patients, citing "the lack of public MRSI datasets suitable for training" ([Dong et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19288)). No deep-learning reconstruction specific to 31P was found. Training would rest on simulation, which makes field strength, pulse profile, T1 weighting and coil into axes of domain shift, and LeJEPA's identifiability guarantee holds only when the true latent variables are Gaussian ([arXiv:2605.26379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26379)). Concentrations and chemical shifts are bounded and structured, so the guarantee does not transfer directly.

Underlying all four is the fact that for an FID the exact world model is already known. The dynamics along the FID axis are linear and time-invariant with known structure, and a physics state-space model with K complex states is the true model. Learning adds value only for what physics does not capture compactly: lineshape distortion, spatial regularity, inter-subject regularity, and the slow physiological time axis.

## Five research directions, ranked by what they preserve

The directions below are ordered by how much of the pipeline's linearity, and therefore its uncertainty machinery, each one keeps. Feasibility is an assessment by the report writer from the notes and the repository structure, not a published result.

| Direction | What is learned | Preserves exact Gaussian posterior? | Risk | Main precedent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Linear-Gaussian temporal state-space model over subspace coefficients in the dynamic script | Transition and process noise (or fixed from kinetics) | Yes, if linear-Gaussian | Low | Kalman filtering; low-rank tensor dynamic 31P |
| 2. Diagnostic experiment: JEPA on simulated 31P FIDs | Encoder and linear predictor | Not applicable (no reconstruction) | Low | Littwin et al.; HSVD |
| 3. Embedding as side information: graph edge weights, outlier flags | Encoder only | Yes, conditional on the weights | Low to medium | JEPA-SCORE; anatomical edge weights in SPICE |
| 4. JEPA-pretrained encoder plus decoder as manifold constraint beside V | Encoder, decoder | No | Medium | Lam-group autoencoder constraint; RAIISE |
| 5. Measurement-domain JEPA and prediction-error energy as regulariser | Encoder, predictor | No | High | None |

**Direction 1** is the world-model idea in its most defensible form. Let the state be the spatial coefficient maps at each repetition, c_t, with a transition c_{t+1} = A c_t + w_t and the existing SPICE forward model as the observation equation. With Gaussian process noise this is a Kalman smoother over repetitions. It replaces the independent per-repetition solves, pools SNR along time, and remains a linear-Gaussian problem, so the Hessian-based covariance extends naturally; that last point is an inference from the model's structure. A learned or kinetic nonlinear transition (mono-exponential PCr recovery, a latent ODE) is the natural second step, at the cost of exactness. The repository's default rank and regularisation settings give an immediate baseline.

**Direction 2** tests the report's central theoretical claims at almost no cost, using the existing synthetic FID generators. Train a small JEPA with a linear, unconstrained complex predictor on delay-embedded simulated FIDs and ask three questions: do the predictor's eigenvalues match HSVD poles; at what per-peak SNR does a resonance vanish from the embedding, and does the threshold follow SNR/(1+SNR); and can a linear probe recover the Pi shift? Compare SIGReg against VICReg regularisation, and against a masked autoencoder to test the ρ-versus-λ ordering. The notes found no JEPA experiment on FIDs or NMR time series of any kind, so even a negative result fills a documented gap.

**Direction 3** keeps the solver untouched. The spatial regulariser already takes weight matrices; embedding similarity between voxels' adjoint or first-pass spectra can supply or modulate those weights, and an embedding-density score can relax the prior in atypical voxels. The reconstruction stays linear given the weights. The weights now depend on the data, however, so the reported covariance is conditional on them, and that dependence needs testing.

**Direction 4** is the direct comparison with existing MRSI learned priors: add a penalty pulling each voxel's FID toward the range of an encoder-decoder pair alongside the subspace term, and compare JEPA pretraining with ordinary autoencoder pretraining at equal latent dimension. The 2026 evidence favours a hybrid over vanilla I-JEPA, meaning latent prediction plus a reconstruction or dense term, with the physics signal model as decoder where possible. Splitting the latent into a physical part (frequencies, dampings, amplitudes) and a residual part follows the pattern of physics-structured JEPA preprints ([Phys-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16076)).

**Direction 5** is the formal meeting point of the two self-supervised traditions. Measurement-splitting methods such as SSDU predict held-out measurements in data space under a known forward model ([Yaman et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.09116)); JEPA predicts held-out embeddings. A JEPA whose masking acts on k-space rings, repetitions or coils, with the forward operator in the loop, does not exist in the literature. It would also have no convergence or stability theory, because the resulting energy is non-convex. Independent-noise views are abundant in MRSI (separate averages, coils, repetitions) and remove the static-distractor problem for noise, though not for static nuisance signals.

Every direction needs the same validation protocol, and it should be fixed before any model is trained. Insert synthetic low-amplitude peaks and shifted or split Pi peaks into realistic simulations; include pathology-like spectra outside the training distribution; report bias and variance of fitted amplitudes against Cramér-Rao bounds across SNR and at the edges of parameter space. Image-quality metrics and apparent SNR are precisely the measures on which learned MRS methods have looked better than they are. A hybrid of network and least-squares fitting has been shown to reduce training-set bias relative to pure regression ([Merkofer et al., ISMRM 2024](https://cds.ismrm.org/protected/24MProceedings/PDFfiles/1207_HPBSDoQCw.html)), which supports ending every pipeline in a physics-model fit of data-consistent signals.

## Conclusion

Applying world-model thinking to MRS does not uncover a new method so much as it places SPICE on a map. SPICE with a simulated basis is already a hand-built latent-variable model, with a linear decoder (V), a physics observation operator, and no dynamics; HSVD is already a linear latent predictor. On that map, JEPA is the architecture that removes the one component reconstruction cannot do without, and the most useful result of the analysis is a specific, testable prediction about which resonances a latent-predictive encoder keeps as SNR falls. The missing piece in this repository is the transition model along the repetition axis, and the tool that fills it is a state-space model of the Kalman kind.

The repository's distinctive asset is exact uncertainty, and learned priors are weakest there. The combination defines the opportunity: a temporal or similarity-guided extension that stays linear-Gaussian would bring learned structure into 31P MRSI with error bars that still mean what they say, which none of the existing learned MRSI priors offers. The limits of this assessment should be stated plainly. The underlying sources were checked mostly at abstract level; the Prony equivalence and the SNR-ordered retention rule are derivations that no published work has confirmed; and the absence of prior JEPA-for-MRS work rests on a limited set of searches. Direction 2 would settle the theoretical claims within days of compute and is the natural first experiment.
