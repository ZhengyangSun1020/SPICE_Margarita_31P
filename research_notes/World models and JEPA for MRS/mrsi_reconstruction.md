## 0. What the local repository (/home/user/SPICE_Margarita_31P) does

### Takeaway
The repo is a classical, physics-based SPICE pipeline: a linear spectral subspace (SVD of synthetic training FIDs) plus a B0-aware NUFFT forward model and graph-Laplacian spatial regularisation solved by conjugate gradients, followed by FSL-MRS fitting and Laplace/Monte-Carlo uncertainty propagation. It contains no learned (neural) prior anywhere; all priors are linear-subspace, quadratic-spatial, or parametric-fit priors.

### Cited Findings

The SPICE reference it cites is PubMed 31483526 (labelled "Liang 2020"). — `/home/user/SPICE_Margarita_31P/README.md`
- [R] The README text is written for *in vivo* brain 2D 1H-MRSI (preprocessing includes MORSE-PI coil sensitivities, B0 map estimation, L2 lipid removal; `basis/` holds 1H metabolite JSONs such as NAA, Cr, Glu, GABA plus `SS_training.csv`). The 31P work lives in top-level `scripts/` files that the README does not document. — `/home/user/SPICE_Margarita_31P/README.md`, `/home/user/SPICE_Margarita_31P/basis/`
- [R] 31P scripts: `05c_spice_recon_31p.py` (2D SPICE), `05d_spice_recon_31p_3d.py` ("31P CONCEPT 3D CRT SPICE reconstruction with optional 3D spatial regularization", handles interleaved 1H/31P and pure 31P Siemens twix data; example uses a liver scan with 30 rings and a subspace source named `pk-7t-liver-3tpulse`), `05e_spice_dynamic_31p.py`, `06c_iterative_recon_31p_3d.py` (stack-of-rings CRT, exact Gram matrix E^H E, CG), `07b_interleaved_recon_31p_3d.py` (per-coil recon then wSVD coil combination, mirroring an OXSA/MATLAB routine), `08_fsl_mrsi_fit_31p.py`. — `/home/user/SPICE_Margarita_31P/scripts/`

### Inferences
- Because the reconstruction is linear in the data given V (a regularised least-squares problem), the posterior is Gaussian and the repo can compute an exact covariance. Any nonlinear learned prior (autoencoder, diffusion, JEPA-style encoder) would break this property; that is the main thing the user would be trading away.
- The two natural insertion points for a learned or predictive prior are (a) the spectral model (replace or augment the SVD basis V) and (b) the missing temporal model in the dynamic 31P script, where frames are currently solved independently.
- Simulation infrastructure already exists (synthetic 31P FID generators, phantom and B0 simulators in `utils/signal.py` and `utils/simulation.py`), so simulation-based pretraining is feasible without new tooling.

## 1. Forward model of MRSI and why the inverse problem is ill-posed

### Takeaway
MRSI measures a (k,t)-space signal that is the spatial Fourier transform of a spatiospectral function modulated by B0-induced phase, corrupted by nuisance signals and noise; it is ill-posed because the target is high-dimensional (3 spatial + 1 spectral, plus time for dynamic scans) while SNR and scan time permit only limited, low-resolution k-space coverage. 31P and other X-nuclei make this worse through lower sensitivity, though they avoid the water/lipid nuisance problem of 1H.

### Cited Findings
- [M] Standard model: d(k,t) = ∫ ρ(r,t) · exp(−i2π Δf(r) t) · exp(−i2π k·r) dr + nuisance(k,t) + noise, where ρ(r,t) is the spatially resolved FID (its temporal Fourier transform is the spectrum), Δf(r) is the B0 field offset, and coil sensitivities multiply ρ for multichannel data. This is the formulation used throughout the SPICE literature and reviewed in Lam, Peng, Liang, "High-Dimensional MR Spatiospectral Imaging by Integrating Physics-Based Modeling and Data-Driven Machine Learning: Current progress and future directions", IEEE Signal Processing Magazine 40(2):101-115, March 2023. — [PubMed record 37538148](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/bvsvs/resource/pt/mdl-37538148); [DOI 10.1109/MSP.2022.3215288](https://unpaywall.org/10.1109%2FMSP.2022.3215288)
- [S] The same review frames the field's goal as recovering "high-dimensional spatiospectral functions from limited data" by exploiting prior information from MR physics, and describes conventional MRSI as "a slow, low-SNR, and poor-resolution modality"; it also covers dynamic MRSI and quantification. — [PubMed record 37538148](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/bvsvs/resource/pt/mdl-37538148)
- [R] The local code implements exactly this structure: a NUFFT spatial encoding operator F, a per-voxel B0 phase matrix over the FID time axis (`Calc_B0_matrix(df_high, taxis)`), and a low-resolution B0 map extrapolated to the high-resolution grid by polynomial fit. — `/home/user/SPICE_Margarita_31P/utils/recon.py`
- [S] Nuisance signals in 1H-MRSI: water and subcutaneous lipid are orders of magnitude larger than metabolites; at ultra-high field, "inhomogeneity and high SAR ... make lipid and water suppression during acquisition prohibitive and inefficient", motivating post-hoc removal (HLSVD + L2, or learned removal). — [Weiser et al., WALINET, arXiv:2410.00746](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00746)
- [S] Low metabolite concentration forces low spatial resolution: "MRSI is usually acquired at low resolution because metabolite concentrations are low". — [Dong et al., FTDDM, arXiv:2410.19288](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19288)
- [S] 31P specifically: whole-brain mapping of low-concentration 31P metabolites such as NAD "could take prohibitively long scan times on the order of hours" without denoising. — [Frontiers in Neuroscience 2024, 10.3389/fnins.2024.1389111](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2024.1389111/epub)
- [S] Typical 31P voxel sizes remain centimetre scale even at 7T: the SPICE 31P abstract reports a nominal in vivo voxel of 9.1 × 9.1 × 16 mm³ with k-space weighting. — [Wiesner et al., ISMRM 2021 #0235](https://archive.ismrm.org/2021/0235.html)
- [S] Limited k-space coverage is a design choice driven by SNR: density-weighted concentric-ring (CRT) 31P-MRSI at 7T measures 3D cardiac PCr/ATP in 2.5 min, 2.6 times faster than the minimum for acquisition-weighted phase-encoded CSI, or gives a 1.7-times smaller nominal voxel in equal time. — [Clarke, Hingerl, Strasser, Bogner, Valkovič, Rodgers, NMR in Biomedicine 36:e4813](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:bb8042bc-f26b-4bc7-bbb1-661601184e62)

### Inferences
- Sources of ill-posedness, combining the above: (1) dimensionality versus measurement count (sparse (k,t) sampling, truncated k-space giving a broad point-spread function and lipid ringing in 1H); (2) noise-dominated data (the unregularised inverse is unbiased but useless); (3) model mismatch entering multiplicatively through B0 (broadening and shifts that vary per voxel, so a single global spectral basis is only approximately valid); (4) nuisance components with dynamic range far above metabolites (1H); (5) for dynamic scans, each time frame has only a fraction of the SNR.
- For 31P the dominant difficulty is SNR and spatial resolution, not nuisance removal or spectral overlap: the spectrum is sparse (roughly 7 to 12 resonances over about 30 ppm), which makes low-rank spectral models very effective, but chemical-shift variation (the pH-dependent Pi shift) and B0 shifts are informative parameters that a too-rigid subspace or learned prior can distort.

### Gaps
- I could not open the Lam/Peng/Liang review to quote its equations; the model above is standard but written from memory.
- No source was retrieved quantifying the 31P versus 1H sensitivity gap. Commonly quoted reasons (gyromagnetic ratio about 40% of 1H, millimolar concentrations, long T1 for PCr) are [M] and unsourced here.

## 2. SPICE: subspace model, basis learning, coefficient estimation, 31P applications

### Takeaway
SPICE represents the spatiospectral function as a low-rank (partially separable) or union-of-subspaces expansion whose spectral basis is fixed in advance (from navigator "D1" data, from training data, or from physics-based simulation), reducing reconstruction to a well-conditioned linear least-squares problem for spatial coefficients with B0 correction and spatial regularisation. For 31P it has been used mainly as a denoising/SNR-enhancing reconstruction (7T brain, NAD mapping, low-rank tensor dynamic 31P in animals); a peer-reviewed human dynamic 31P SPICE creatine-kinase paper was not found.

### Cited Findings
- [M] Model: ρ(r,t) = Σ_{l=1..L} c_l(r) φ_l(t) (partial separability; Casorati matrix of rank L), extended to a union of subspaces ρ = Σ_m Σ_l c_{l,m}(r) φ_{l,m}(t) with separate subspaces for metabolites, macromolecules, water and lipids. Reconstruction: min_C ‖d − Ω F B (C Φ)‖² + λ‖W D C‖², with B the B0 phase operator and W edge weights from anatomical images. Original papers: Liang, ISBI 2007 (partial separability); Lam and Liang, "A subspace approach to high-resolution spectroscopic imaging", MRM 2014; Lam, Ma, Clifford, Johnson, Liang, MRM 2016. — the 2016 paper is believed to be [PMC4848237](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4848237) (returned by search, not opened)
- [S] "A key feature of SPICE is the use of a subspace model for both data acquisition and image reconstruction." — [Lam, Li, Guo, Clifford, Liang, "Ultrafast magnetic resonance spectroscopic imaging using SPICE with learned subspaces", MRM (2019 online / 2020 issue)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6824949)
- [S] That paper introduced **learned spectral subspaces** estimated from training data so that subject-specific navigator data are no longer required, mainly for 1H-MRSI. This is the paper the local README cites. — [PMC6824949](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6824949)
- [M] Three generations of basis estimation: (a) hybrid acquisition, where a low-resolution, high-SNR, full-spectral "D1" dataset gives the basis by SVD and a sparse, high-resolution "D2" dataset gives the spatial coefficients; (b) learned subspaces, where spectral parameters (concentrations, linewidths, shifts) are fitted on training subjects, their empirical distributions are sampled, spectra are synthesised with quantum-mechanically simulated metabolite basis functions, and the SVD of the synthetic ensemble gives V; (c) probabilistic subspace models with priors on coefficients. Verify against PMC6824949 and the SPM review. — [PMC6824949](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6824949); [PubMed 37538148](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/bvsvs/resource/pt/mdl-37538148)
- [R] The local repo follows variant (b) in simplified form for 31P: 5000 synthetic FIDs from a peak model, SVD, truncate to rank about 6, or alternatively variant (a)-like "average-adjoint" where the time-averaged data provide the basis. — `/home/user/SPICE_Margarita_31P/scripts/05e_spice_dynamic_31p.py`
- [S] 31P at 3T: Ma, Lam, Ning, Clifford, Larsen, Liang applied a subspace approach to high-resolution 31P-MRSI (ISMRM 2016 abstract). — [ISMRM 2016 #3956](https://cds.ismrm.org/protected/16MProceedings/PDFfiles/3956.html)
- [S] 31P at 7T: Wiesner, Guo, Li, Zhao, Liang, Zhu, Chen, "High-resolution 3D Phosphorus Metabolic Imaging of the Human Brain at 7T using SPICE" (ISMRM 2021 #0235): phantom and healthy-volunteer data on a Siemens 7T with a dual-channel 31P-1H coil; SPICE used as denoising; nominal voxel 9.1 × 9.1 × 16 mm³; conclusion that UHF 31P MRSI plus SPICE denoising enables whole-brain phosphorus imaging "within a reasonable acquisition time". Conference abstract only. — [ISMRM 2021 #0235](https://archive.ismrm.org/2021/0235.html)
- [S] 31P NAD mapping at 7T (University of Minnesota group with SPICE-based processing): 3D 31P MRSI with a "probabilistic subspace model"; the ISMRM 2023 abstract reports over 3-fold noise reduction and the first whole-brain NAD quantification. — [ISMRM 2023 #3291](https://cds.ismrm.org/protected/23MProceedings/PDFfiles/3291_Zb73vCRBK.html); [Frontiers in Neuroscience 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2024.1389111/epub)
- [S] Dynamic 31P: Ma et al., "High-resolution dynamic 31P-MRSI using a low-rank tensor model", MRM 2017: explicit subspace pursuit, with bases estimated from "training" data then a high-resolution function reconstructed from "imaging" data; 30 s per frame; performed at 9.4T (animal). — [British Library record](https://eld.bl.uk/catalog/100047913544_0x000001); probable full text [PMC5132121](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5132121) (not opened)
- [S, detail unverified] A later low-rank tensor 31P method with spiral trajectories and sparse (k, t, T)-space sampling reports sub-centimetre resolution at 30 s temporal resolution in rats and notes that prior knowledge of each metabolite's subspace can be built in. — [PMC7384926](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7384926)
- [S] Low-rank denoising (not full SPICE) for 31P at 7T: in cardiac CRT 31P-MRSI, low-rank denoising "reduced the variance of measured PCr/ATP ratios by 11% across all protocols"; authors note benefit for creatine-kinase rate measurements that need repeated scans. — [Clarke et al., NMR in Biomedicine 36:e4813](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:bb8042bc-f26b-4bc7-bbb1-661601184e62)
- [S] Related abstracts: Korzowski et al. tested whether low-rank denoising can replace signal averaging in volumetric 31P MRSI of calf at 7T; Clarke and Chiew compared low-rank denoising methods on 31P-MRSI of muscle and brain. — [ISMRM 2020 #2966](https://cds.ismrm.org/protected/20MProceedings/PDFfiles/2966.html); [ISMRM 2020 #2988](https://cds.ismrm.org/protected/20MProceedings/PDFfiles/2988.html)
- [S] Reference for human dynamic 31P without subspace modelling: Valkovič et al. 2016, spiral dynamic 31P-MRSI of calf at 7T mapping mitochondrial capacity during plantar-flexion exercise. — [PMC5709247](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5709247) (URL-to-paper mapping inferred from search listing)
- [S] Thesis-level sources for 31P SPICE: a 2022 University of Illinois dissertation states the improved SPICE method "was used for 31P-MRSI at 7T for high resolution mapping of high-energy metabolites". — [IDEALS item 125050](https://ideals.illinois.edu/items/125050/bitstreams/410702/data.pdf)

### Inferences
- In 31P work to date, SPICE's reported gain is mostly SNR (about 3-fold noise reduction in the NAD abstract; 11% variance reduction of PCr/ATP in the modest low-rank cardiac case), not a dramatic nominal-resolution jump as in 1H SPICE. Resolution gains follow indirectly, by making smaller voxels or shorter scans affordable.
- The subspace prior is linear, global (one basis for all voxels) and easy to analyse: bias arises only from signal components outside span(V) and from the spatial regulariser. This is the baseline any learned prior must beat, and the reason linear-subspace uncertainty quantification (the local repo's focus) is tractable.
- The existing dynamic 31P subspace literature models time with low-rank tensors (a linear temporal subspace). Nobody appears to have used a learned *predictive* dynamics model for PCr depletion/recovery or saturation-transfer kinetics.

### Gaps
- I did not find a peer-reviewed journal article specifically titled as 31P SPICE at 7T in human brain from the Liang/Lam group (only the ISMRM 2021 abstract, the 2022 thesis, and the Minnesota-led NAD paper). A 2023 to 2026 MRM or NMR in Biomedicine version may exist; the search did not surface it.
- No human dynamic 31P SPICE creatine-kinase (e.g. saturation-transfer) paper was found. Reported as not found, not as non-existent.
- Typical 1H SPICE headline numbers (roughly 2 to 3 mm resolution in 5 to 8 min at 3T, from memory) could not be confirmed here because PMC was unreachable.
- Citation details for Liang 2007, Lam and Liang 2014, and Lam et al. 2016 are from memory.

## 3. Deep-learning priors for MRSI and MRS (2019 to 2026)

### Takeaway
Learned priors in MRSI fall into five families: (i) learned nonlinear low-dimensional spectral manifolds (autoencoders) used as constraints in physics-based reconstruction, (ii) end-to-end or physics-informed networks for fast reconstruction and nuisance removal (Deep-ER, WALINET), (iii) generative super-resolution of metabolite maps (diffusion), (iv) denoising including self-supervised variants, and (v) learned quantification. As of late 2026, foundation-model-style pretraining for MRS exists only at conference-abstract level, and nearly all of this work is 1H brain; I found no deep-learning reconstruction paper specific to 31P MRSI.

### Cited Findings

**(i) Learned nonlinear low-dimensional models (Lam group)**
- [S, author order unverified] "Constrained Magnetic Resonance Spectroscopic Imaging by Learning Nonlinear Low-Dimensional Models", IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging, 2020 (authors Lam, Li, Peng; sources disagree on order). A deep autoencoder is trained to "capture the low-dimensional manifold where the high-dimensional spectroscopic signals reside"; the learned model is then used as a constraint in reconstruction. — [University of Iowa record](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Constrained-Magnetic-Resonance-Spectroscopic-Imaging-by/9984446409702771)
- [M] Formulation to verify in that paper: min_X ‖d − A(X)‖² + λ1 Σ_n ‖x_n − D(E(x_n))‖² + λ2 R_spatial(X), where E and D are the trained encoder and decoder, each voxel FID x_n is pulled toward the autoencoder's range, and training spectra are synthesised from quantum-mechanical basis functions with sampled concentrations, lineshapes and shifts. The argument is that parameters such as linewidth and frequency shift enter nonlinearly, so a nonlinear model needs fewer dimensions than a linear subspace for the same accuracy. — [University of Iowa record](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Constrained-Magnetic-Resonance-Spectroscopic-Imaging-by/9984446409702771)
- [S] Li, Wang, Sun, Lam, "Separation of Metabolites and Macromolecules for Short-TE 1H-MRSI Using Learned Component-Specific Representations": mixed unsupervised and supervised strategy to learn metabolite-specific and macromolecule-specific low-dimensional representations with deep autoencoders. — [PMC8049099](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8049099/)
- [S] Li, Wang, Lam: deep complex convolutional autoencoder learning a nonlinear low-dimensional model of multi-TE spectra for SNR enhancement and molecular-signal separation. — [NSF PAR 10394735](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10394735)
- [S] RAIISE (Li et al., published February 2025): jointly learns a nonlinear representation of spectroscopic signals and a network "projector" that recovers low-dimensional embeddings from noisy data, for fast constrained reconstruction. — [Illinois Experts record](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/joint-learning-of-nonlinear-representation-and-projection-for-fas/)
- [S] Thesis: Yahang Li, "High-dimensional MR spectroscopic imaging integrating physics-based modeling and machine learning", University of Illinois, 2023. — [IDEALS item 129282](https://ideals.illinois.edu/items/129282)

**(ii) Reconstruction and nuisance-removal networks (Weiser, Andronesi, Bogner and colleagues)**
- [S] Deep-ER (Weiser et al., NeuroImage 2025; arXiv:2409.18303): network with interlaced convolutional layers and joint dual-space (k-space and image) features for undersampled non-Cartesian ECCENTRIC 1H-MRSI at 7T, 3.4 mm isotropic, acquisition 4:11 to 9:21 min:s; trained on 21 subjects, tested on 6; each time point's k-space volume is reconstructed separately; reported 600-fold faster than iterative Total Generalized Variation reconstruction, with higher SNR and lower Cramér-Rao bounds. Numbers are from the preprint/abstract. — [arXiv:2409.18303](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.18303); [PMC11952141](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11952141/)
- [S] Deep-ERx2 (ISMRM 2025 #4606): extension to both 3T and 7T, about 1 min reconstruction. — [ISMRM 2025 #4606](https://archive.ismrm.org/2025/4606.html)
- [S] WALINET (Weiser et al.; arXiv:2410.00746, journal version in PMC): modified Y-Net CNN with two encoders that predicts the water and lipid contamination spectrum, which is subtracted from the measured spectrum; 25 to 45-fold lipid and 34 to 53-fold water suppression in brain; 8 s for whole-brain high-resolution MRSI versus 42 min for HLSVD + L2; code public. A lipid-only variant is called LIPNET. — [arXiv:2410.00746](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00746); [PMC11782715](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11782715/)
- [S] WALINET+ (arXiv:2512.14929, December 2025, preprint): also removes water sidebands from water-unsuppressed 7T ECCENTRIC MRSI; with Deep-ER, yields simultaneous metabolic, QSM and myelin-water-fraction mapping at 2 mm isotropic in 12 min. — [arXiv:2512.14929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.14929)

**(iii) Generative super-resolution and diffusion**
- [S] Dong et al., Flow-based Truncated Denoising Diffusion Model (FTDDM) for super-resolution MRSI (arXiv:2410.19288; Medical Image Analysis): truncated diffusion chain with a normalizing flow for the truncated steps, over 9-fold faster sampling than baseline diffusion, conditioned on upscaling factor, supports uncertainty estimation and adjustable sharpness; trained on in vivo 1H-MRSI from 25 high-grade glioma patients, citing "the lack of public MRSI datasets suitable for training"; neuroradiologist evaluation. Operates on metabolite maps, not (k,t) data. — [arXiv:2410.19288](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19288)
- [S] Precursor: Iqbal et al. 2019, Dense U-Net super-resolution of 1H-MRSI maps with T1 guidance, trained on metabolite maps synthesised from MRI rather than measured MRSI. — [PMC6794570](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6794570/)
- [S, venue unverified] A conditional DDPM study for IDH-mutant glioma reconstructs high-resolution metabolite maps from low-resolution input. — [Weill Cornell record](https://vivo.weill.cornell.edu/display/pubid39353335)
- [S] "Missing Data Estimation for MR Spectroscopic Imaging via Mask-Free Deep Learning Methods" (arXiv:2505.06811, 2025): 3D U-Net fills missing voxels in metabolite maps; states "Deep learning remains largely underutilized in the domain of MRSI." — [arXiv:2505.06811](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.06811v1)

**(iv) Denoising, including self-supervised**
- [S] Zhao et al., ISMRM 2023 #0955: denoiser for MRSI reconstruction trained with Noise2Void on noisy data alone; reports SNR enhancement and "tissue-specific spectral feature preservation" in simulation and in vivo. Abstract only. — [ISMRM 2023 #0955](https://cds.ismrm.org/protected/23MProceedings/PDFfiles/0955_AlCSaCQkl.html)
- [S, URL mapping uncertain] Wang et al. 2023: stacked autoencoder for MRS denoising trained both self-supervised and supervised; NAA chemical shift in denoised spectra matched high-SNR spectra; small study (phantom plus three subjects). — [PMC10872746](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10872746)
- [S] Counter-evidence: Dziadosz, Rizzo, Kyathanahally, Kreis, "Denoising single MR spectra by deep learning: Miracle or mirage?", MRM 2023 (see section 5). — [BORIS PDF](https://boris.unibe.ch/183536/1/Magnetic_Resonance_in_Med_-_2023_-_Dziadosz_-_Denoising_single_MR_spectra_by_deep_learning_Miracle_or_mirage.pdf)

**(v) Quantification**
- [S] Rizzo, Dziadosz, Kyathanahally, Shamaei, Kreis, "Quantification of MR spectra by deep learning in an idealized setting", MRM 2023 (see section 5 for findings). — [BORIS 176145](https://boris.unibe.ch/176145)
- [S] Merkofer et al., ISMRM 2024 #1207: supervised regression is most accurate in-distribution but shows the most data bias; a hybrid network plus least-squares fitting "significantly reduces training bias" and is more reliable out of distribution. — [ISMRM 2024 #1207](https://cds.ismrm.org/protected/24MProceedings/PDFfiles/1207_HPBSDoQCw.html)

### Inferences
- The closest existing thing to a "latent-space prior" in MRSI is the Lam-group autoencoder constraint and RAIISE's learned projector: both learn an embedding of single-voxel spectra from physics simulation and use it inside a data-consistent reconstruction. - No INR-for-MRSI paper and no published (non-abstract) MRS foundation model were surfaced; both look like open territory as of late 2026.

## 4. Has anyone applied JEPA, masked latent prediction, contrastive/self-supervised representation learning, or world-model ideas to MRS/MRSI, MRI reconstruction, or dynamic MR?

### Takeaway
**Nothing was found applying JEPA, latent predictive learning, or "world model" framing to MRS, MRSI, 31P, or MR fingerprinting.** JEPA has reached MRI only as an image-level representation-learning method for structural brain MRI (2026 preprints), and masked/contrastive pretraining has reached k-space only for cardiac cine; none of these is used as a reconstruction prior for spectroscopy.

### Inferences
- The gap is real on three levels: (1) no JEPA or latent-predictive model on spectra or (k,t) MRSI data; (2) no JEPA used as a *reconstruction prior* in any MR modality (existing MR JEPA work targets classification or synthesis from clean images); (3) no action-conditioned or dynamics-predictive ("world model") formulation for dynamic MR, including dynamic 31P.

## 5. Known risks of learned priors in MRSI

### Takeaway
The best-documented risk is regression toward the training distribution: networks look more precise than model fitting at low SNR because uncertain cases are biased toward the training-set mean, and denoised spectra that look better do not quantify better. Scarce in vivo data force reliance on simulation, which introduces domain shift across field strength, sequence, nucleus and pathology; standard uncertainty measures (CRLB) do not transfer to learned estimators.

### Cited Findings
- [S] Rizzo et al., MRM 2023: on simulated spectra, "model fitting is more accurate, although deep learning appears to be more precise at low SNR"; the apparent precision gain "originates from a strong bias for cases with high uncertainty toward the dataset the network has been trained with, tending toward its average value." — [BORIS 176145](https://boris.unibe.ch/176145)
- [S] Rizzo et al., MICCAI 2022 ("Reliability of Quantification Estimates in MR Spectroscopy: CNNs vs Traditional Model Fitting"): deep learning is "a dataset-biased technique"; predictions show "bias and increased uncertainty at the edges of the explored parameter space", persisting even without noise; "training with uniform datasets or if augmented with critical cases showed to be insufficient to prevent biases." Network uncertainty was split into aleatoric (bias and spread of predictions) and epistemic (Monte Carlo dropout) parts and compared with CRLBs. — [Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/978-3-031-16452-1_68); [BORIS 176112](https://boris.unibe.ch/176112)
- [S] Dziadosz et al., MRM 2023: supervised U-Net denoising of simulated brain spectra gives "visually appealing spectra", but "noise removal is inhomogeneous and more efficient for signal-free areas"; conclusion: such denoising "may be useful for display purposes, but do not help quantitative evaluations." — [BORIS PDF](https://boris.unibe.ch/183536/1/Magnetic_Resonance_in_Med_-_2023_-_Dziadosz_-_Denoising_single_MR_spectra_by_deep_learning_Miracle_or_mirage.pdf)
- [S] Review-level statement: for data-driven methods, CRLB-style precision measures are generally biased and alternative uncertainty metrics are hard to validate. — [arXiv:2305.09621](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.09621)
- [S] Mitigation evidence: combining a network with physics-based least-squares fitting reduces training-set bias and improves out-of-distribution behaviour relative to pure regression. — [Merkofer et al., ISMRM 2024 #1207](https://cds.ismrm.org/protected/24MProceedings/PDFfiles/1207_HPBSDoQCw.html)
- [S] Data scarcity: MRSI diffusion work had to build its own dataset of 25 glioma patients because of "the lack of public MRSI datasets suitable for training"; Deep-ER trained on 21 subjects; the first MRSI super-resolution network trained on maps synthesised from MRI, not measured MRSI. — [arXiv:2410.19288](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19288); [arXiv:2409.18303](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.18303); [PMC6794570](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6794570/)
- [S] Dependence on simulation: the Lam-group nonlinear models and SPICE learned subspaces are trained on spectra synthesised from physics (quantum-mechanical basis functions plus empirical parameter distributions) rather than on large measured datasets. — [PMC6824949](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6824949); [University of Iowa record](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Constrained-Magnetic-Resonance-Spectroscopic-Imaging-by/9984446409702771)
- [S] Domain shift across field strength is handled by retraining or explicit multi-field models rather than by generalisation: Deep-ER was 7T-only and needed a separate extension (Deep-ERx2) to cover 3T and 7T. — [ISMRM 2025 #4606](https://archive.ismrm.org/2025/4606.html)
- [S] Validation caveat: reported performance for WALINET and Deep-ER comes from the authors' own simulations and in vivo data; self-supervised MRS denoising evidence rests on a phantom plus three subjects or on conference abstracts. — [arXiv:2410.00746](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00746); [PMC10872746](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10872746); [ISMRM 2023 #0955](https://cds.ismrm.org/protected/23MProceedings/PDFfiles/0955_AlCSaCQkl.html)
- [R] Local contrast: the repo's linear SPICE gives an exact Gaussian posterior covariance and propagates it through fitting, a guarantee that has no established counterpart for learned priors. — `/home/user/SPICE_Margarita_31P/README.md`

### Inferences
- Any prior that works by discarding "unpredictable" content, which is JEPA's design principle, is exposed to the same failure Kreis's group documented: at low SNR the unpredictable part includes the true deviation of a voxel from the population mean. For 31P this is concrete: the Pi chemical shift (pH), a split Pi peak, PCr depletion during exercise, and pathological PME/PDE changes are the scientifically interesting signals and are rare or absent in typical training simulations.
- A defensible design keeps the measurement model in the loop (learned representation as a soft constraint or initialiser with explicit data consistency, as in the autoencoder-constrained and hybrid network-plus-least-squares approaches), and evaluates on quantification bias versus ground truth across SNR and at parameter-space edges, not on visual SNR or image metrics.
- For 31P, in vivo training data are far scarcer than for 1H brain (multi-site 1H datasets of tens of subjects are already considered small), so simulation is the only realistic training source, and field strength (3T vs 7T J-coupling pattern and dispersion), excitation bandwidth/pulse profile, TR/T1 weighting and coil all become domain-shift axes. The repo already encodes one such axis (a subspace source named `pk-7t-liver-3tpulse`, separate 3T/7T bases).
- Uncertainty quantification is the user's own research focus (ISMRM 2026 abstract), and it is exactly what is weakest for learned priors; this is both the main risk and a possible contribution.

### Gaps
- "Hallucination" in the strict sense (a learned MRSI reconstruction inventing a lesion or metabolite peak) was not found documented in a dedicated MRSI study; the evidence found concerns bias toward the training mean and non-uniform denoising in single-voxel 1H MRS. Hallucination evidence from general MRI reconstruction (e.g. fastMRI challenge analyses) was not searched.
- No study was found quantifying learned-prior bias for 31P or any X-nucleus.
- No study was found testing a network trained at one field strength or sequence on another for MRSI reconstruction with quantified degradation; the domain-shift statement rests on the indirect evidence above.
- Full texts of the Rizzo and Dziadosz papers were not opened; quotations are from abstracts as returned by search.
